# AI Tools Available to You

A guide to every AI tool in your Microsoft 365 toolkit — what each one does and how to get the most from it.

---

## Microsoft Copilot (General)

**What it is:** Your everyday AI assistant, built into Microsoft 365. Accessible via copilot.microsoft.com or the Copilot sidebar in M365 apps.

**Best for:**
- Writing and editing drafts (emails, reports, summaries)
- Brainstorming ideas
- Explaining concepts in plain language
- Answering questions
- Reformatting and restructuring content
- Creating plans, frameworks, and outlines

**How to access:**
- Web: [copilot.microsoft.com](https://copilot.microsoft.com)
- In Teams: Click the Copilot icon in any chat
- In Office apps: Click the Copilot button in the ribbon

**Starter prompts:**
- `"Summarize this document in 5 bullet points"`
- `"Help me write a [type of document] for [audience]"`
- `"Brainstorm 10 ideas for [challenge]"`
- `"Explain [topic] to someone with no background in it"`

**Tips:**
- Always give it context: who you are, what you need, who the audience is
- Use follow-up messages to refine output
- It doesn't remember previous conversations — restart if it gets confused

## Copilot in Teams

**What it is:** AI assistance built into Microsoft Teams for meetings and chats.

**Best for:**
- Summarizing what you missed in a meeting
- Getting action items from a meeting
- Catching up on a long Teams chat thread
- Searching for information across your conversations

**How to access:** Teams → open any meeting or chat → click the Copilot icon

**In a live meeting:**
- `"Summarize what's been discussed so far"`
- `"What decisions have been made?"`
- `"List all action items with owners"`
- `"What did [person] say about [topic]?"`

**After a meeting:**
- Open the meeting recording/transcript
- Copilot can generate a full summary, action list, and more

**Tip:** Transcription or recording must be enabled for Copilot to work in meetings. Inform all attendees.

## Copilot in Outlook

**What it is:** AI writing and summarization built into Outlook.

**Best for:**
- Drafting email replies
- Summarizing long email threads
- Improving tone and clarity of emails
- Creating email drafts from scratch

**How to access:** Outlook → compose or open an email → click the Copilot icon

**Key features:**
- "Draft with Copilot" — generates a full email draft
- "Coaching" — gives you feedback on your email's tone, clarity, and length
- "Summarize" — condenses a long thread into key points

**Starter prompts:**
- `"Draft a reply that acknowledges the delay and proposes a new timeline"`
- `"Make this email shorter and more direct"`
- `"Summarize this thread and pull out any action items for me"`

## Copilot Analyst

**What it is:** A powerful tool for analyzing documents, spreadsheets, and data files. You upload files and ask questions in natural language.

**Best for:**
- Analyzing Excel spreadsheets and CSV data
- Summarizing long Word documents or PDFs
- Comparing two documents
- Finding patterns or anomalies in data
- Creating charts and visual summaries from data

**How to access:** [copilot.microsoft.com](https://copilot.microsoft.com) → select Analyst mode, or via the M365 Copilot app

**Getting started:**
1. Open Copilot
2. Attach your file(s)
3. Ask your question in plain English

**Starter prompts:**
- `"What are the top 3 trends in this data?"`
- `"Compare these two documents and list the key differences"`
- `"Summarize the key recommendations in this report"`
- `"Create a chart showing sales by region from this spreadsheet"`
- `"Flag any anomalies or outliers in this data"`

**Supported file types:** Excel (.xlsx, .csv), Word (.docx), PDF, PowerPoint (.pptx), text files

**Tips:**
- Be specific about what you want to know — vague questions get vague answers
- Upload context if needed: "This is a quarterly sales report. The target was £2M."
- Ask follow-up questions to drill deeper into findings
- Great for reports that would take you 30+ minutes to read manually

## Copilot Researcher

**What it is:** An AI research assistant that searches the web and synthesizes information with citations.

**Best for:**
- Research briefings on topics, companies, or markets
- Staying current on trends and news
- Comparing products, competitors, or approaches
- Finding best practices for a process or skill
- Answering "what does the evidence say about X?"

**How to access:** [copilot.microsoft.com](https://copilot.microsoft.com) → select Researcher mode

**Getting started:**
1. Open Copilot Researcher
2. Ask your research question
3. Review the response and citations
4. Ask follow-up questions

**Starter prompts:**
- `"Brief me on the latest trends in [industry] — focus on the last 6 months"`
- `"What are the best practices for [process/skill]? Cite your sources."`
- `"Compare [Option A] vs [Option B] on: cost, features, pros, cons"`
- `"What do experts currently say about [topic]? Include different perspectives."`
- `"Research [company name] — what are their strategic priorities right now?"`

**Tips:**
- Always check the citations — click through to verify key claims
- Ask for "recent" information to get current results
- Use it to save hours of manual research, not to replace your judgment
- Ask it to format as a 1-page brief for easy sharing

## Copilot Studio (Agents)

**What it is:** A no-code/low-code tool for building custom AI agents (bots) that can be deployed in Teams, SharePoint, or websites.

**Best for:**
- Building a custom AI assistant for your team
- Creating a bot that knows your company policies, FAQs, or procedures
- Automating repetitive Q&A tasks
- Helping new starters with self-service information

**How to access:** [copilotstudio.microsoft.com](https://copilotstudio.microsoft.com) (requires appropriate license)

**What an agent can do:**
- Answer questions based on documents you provide (knowledge base)
- Follow conversation flows you design
- Integrate with Microsoft Graph, SharePoint, and other systems
- Be embedded in Teams as a chat bot

**Building your first agent — quick steps:**
1. Go to copilotstudio.microsoft.com
2. Click **+ New agent**
3. Describe what your agent should do (Copilot helps you set it up!)
4. Add knowledge sources: SharePoint pages, Word docs, PDFs
5. Write instructions for how it should behave
6. Test it in the built-in preview
7. Publish to Teams

**Ideas for agents you could build:**
- "AI FAQ Bot" — answers common Copilot questions
- "Policy Helper" — answers HR/IT policy questions
- "Prompt Coach" — helps users write better prompts
- "Onboarding Assistant" — guides new starters through their first week

**Tip:** Start with a simple agent (5–10 FAQs), test it, then expand. Don't try to build everything at once.
