# Responsible AI at [Company Name]

AI tools like Microsoft Copilot are powerful — but with great power comes responsibility. This page sets out how we use AI tools safely, ethically, and effectively.

---

## Our Guiding Principles

We follow Microsoft's six Responsible AI principles:

| Principle | What It Means For Us |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Fairness** | AI output must not discriminate. Review outputs involving people for bias. |
| **Reliability & Safety** | Don't rely solely on AI for critical decisions. Always apply human judgment. |
| **Privacy & Security** | Never input confidential, personal, or sensitive data into Copilot. |
| **Inclusiveness** | Use AI to make content more accessible, not to exclude or make assumptions about people. |
| **Transparency** | Be honest when AI helped create content. Disclose when appropriate. |
| **Accountability** | You are responsible for what you send, publish, or decide — even if AI helped. |

---

## The Golden Rule

> **Treat AI output like a first draft from a smart but junior colleague — always review before using.**

---

## What You SHOULD Do

- Use Copilot to draft emails, reports, summaries, and agendas
- Use it to research topics and synthesize information
- Use it to save time on repetitive or formulaic writing tasks
- Review, edit, and take ownership of all AI-generated content
- Experiment and share what works with the team via the Prompt Library
- Report any concerns or unexpected outputs to [AI Guru name / contact]

---

## What You Should NOT Do

### Never Input These Into Copilot

| Data Type | Examples | Why |
|-----------|----------|-----|
| Personal data | Names + contact details, staff salaries, health info | GDPR / data protection |
| Client confidential | Client names, contracts, financial data | NDA / confidentiality obligations |
| Company confidential | Unannounced strategies, M&A info, board materials | Business risk |
| Credentials | Passwords, API keys, access tokens | Security risk |
| Legal privileged | Legal advice, litigation documents | Privilege risk |

> If in doubt, don't paste it in. Summarize or paraphrase the content instead.

## Using AI in Meetings

When using Copilot in Teams meetings:
- Inform all attendees that recording/transcription and AI summary are enabled
- External guests must be notified and consent before joining a meeting with transcription on
- Meeting summaries and transcripts should be treated as confidential
- Don't share AI meeting summaries externally without review

---

## Disclosing AI Use

When to disclose that AI helped:
- **Client-facing content**: Consider adding a note that AI tools were used in drafting
- **Published content** (intranet, external comms): Follow communications team guidance
- **Reports and analysis**: Note that AI tools were used for initial drafting/analysis
- **Internal emails and documents**: Disclosure is optional but encouraged for transparency

Check your communications and legal teams for the current company policy.

---

## Quality & Accuracy

AI can get things wrong. Always verify:

1. **Facts and statistics** — Copilot can hallucinate figures. Cross-check any numbers.
2. **Legal or regulatory information** — Always consult qualified professionals.
3. **Technical accuracy** — Have a subject matter expert review technical content.
4. **Tone and cultural sensitivity** — AI doesn't always get tone right. Read it aloud.
5. **Links and references** — Don't trust links generated by AI — always check they work and are credible.

---

## Copyright and Intellectual Property

- AI-generated content is generally not protected by copyright (varies by jurisdiction)
- Be mindful of using AI to closely mimic or reproduce existing copyrighted works
- Don't use AI to create content that could be confused with official publications (regulators, governments, etc.)
- If in doubt, consult the legal team
